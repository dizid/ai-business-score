AI Visibility Engineering

How to get a brand, product, or company mentioned, cited, and recommended by ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews, Perplexity, Claude, and Grok — treated as an engineering discipline, not a content-marketing trend.

Be extractable on your own pages, corroborable on other people's pages, and the named answer to a named constraint.

Built for

  • Founders & SaaS teams
  • SEO professionals moving into GEO
  • Growth marketers
  • Developers & content strategists
  • Agencies building an AI-visibility service line

By the end, you can

  • Measure how visible a company is inside AI answers
  • Diagnose why competitors get mentioned instead
  • Engineer content and off-site signals AI can cite
  • Build entity authority around a brand, product, or person
  • Move from citation to recommendation
  • Run experiments and prove what actually worked
How to use this course
  1. Read Modules 01–03 once. That's the model — the rest of the course is just applying it.
  2. Skip straight to Module 20 and run the 90-day system in order. Baseline before you change anything.
  3. Do not start by rewriting blog posts. Rewriting content before you've measured a baseline and mapped the entity graph is the single most common wasted effort in this field.
  4. Every intervention in Parts II and III is tagged with the layer it targets — Weights Retrieval Entity Graph Measurement — so you always know what you're actually trying to move.
Part I

The Model

Three modules, read once. Everything downstream is an application of the ideas here — the shift from ranking pages to being the entity an AI system trusts enough to name.

Module 01Framework

The New Search Model

Ranking #1 on Google no longer guarantees a mention. The optimization target itself has changed.

Two different pipelines

Traditional search

Query → ranking → click → website. The user does the synthesis. Your job is to win the click.

AI search

Question → retrieval → candidate sources → interpretation → synthesis → answer → citations / recommendations. The model does the synthesis. Your job is to be trustworthy material for it to synthesize with.

A page can rank #1 and contribute almost nothing to an AI answer. A small site can dominate an AI answer if its information is specific, easy to extract, authoritative, independently corroborated, current, and precisely relevant to the question asked. The SEO fundamentals — crawlability, indexing, clarity, authority — still support AI grounding and citation. They're necessary. They're no longer sufficient.

Three outcomes — don't conflate them

LevelWhat it looks likeWhat it means
1 · Mention"Another option is Brand X."The entity is known and recalled.
2 · Citation"According to Brand X…"Your own page was used as evidence.
3 · Recommendation"For your situation, I'd recommend Brand X."The model is confident enough to stake an answer on you.

Target order: Recommendation > Mention > Citation. Recommendation is the most commercially valuable and requires the strongest entity-level evidence — a citation alone doesn't buy it.

Module 02WeightsRetrievalEntity Graph

How AI Decides What to Say

AI systems aren't ranking your website. They're solving discovery, understanding, relevance, trust, evidence, selection, and recommendation — in that order, for every query.

The two engines behind every answer

Parametric memory

Stored neural-network weights, learned from a multi-terabyte training corpus (Common Crawl, Wikipedia, academic datasets). This is what dictates a brand's intrinsic, default authority and whether the model recognizes the entity at all. Updates every 6–18 months, on a training cycle.

Non-parametric RAG

Real-time vector search over live indices. Chunks, re-ranks, and summarizes current documents to answer specific, time-sensitive, or granular queries. Updates continuously, on the crawl cycle.

The 3-stage RAG execution pipeline

  1. Chunking & vector embedding — target pages are parsed into discrete text chunks, typically 200–450 tokens, and mapped into a high-dimensional embedding space. Chunk coherence determines match accuracy.
  2. Cross-encoder re-ranking — candidate chunks are filtered through a secondary model evaluating information density, factual specificity, context clarity, and domain authority.
  3. Citation allocation & synthesis — the generator selects the passages that resolve the prompt with minimal ambiguity, and cites the URLs that provide the most unassailable factual consensus.
DimensionParametric foundationRAG engine
Update frequency6–18 month training intervalsContinuous, real-time crawl cycles
Core sourcesWikipedia, Wikidata, books, ArXiv, Common CrawlWeb indices, community forums, live news, technical docs
Optimization objectiveEntity co-occurrence density across knowledge basesPassage extractability, structured schemas, fresh verification
Primary leverHigh-authority digital PR, Wikipedia / Wikidata presenceAnswer-block layout, structured comparison tables, live feeds

The AI Visibility Equation

Visibility = Discoverability × Extractability × Relevance × Authority × Evidence × Entity Clarity × Freshness

It's multiplicative, not additive. If any factor approaches zero, visibility collapses — an excellent company with excellent content and a blocked crawler is practically invisible to that engine, full stop. No amount of reputation compensates for a factor at zero.

The three layers

Every tactic in this course targets one of three layers. Name the layer before you ship the tactic — it's how you avoid busywork that feels productive but moves nothing:

Weights

What the model already "knows" from training. Moved by sustained, independently-authored mentions before the next training cutoff — not by anything you publish today.

Retrieval

What live search/RAG pulls in right now. Moved by extractable on-page content, freshness, and crawler access — this is where most short-term wins live.

Entity Graph

Whether the model can resolve "you" as one unambiguous thing across sources. Moved by consistent identity, `sameAs` linking, and a single canonical description.

Module 03Entity Graph

Entity Engineering

AI systems don't need to know "Acme makes software." They need to resolve Acme as a specific node with specific, verifiable edges.

The graph you're building isn't a metaphor — construct it as an actual spreadsheet, then attach evidence to every row:

Entity consistency audit

Check the description of the company across every surface it appears on: website, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, directories, review sites, industry publications, Reddit, YouTube, podcasts, GitHub, press releases, partner sites, comparison sites.

Fragmented identitySite A: "AI cybersecurity platform." Site B: "digital risk monitoring tool." Site C: "brand protection SaaS." The model has to reconcile three descriptions before it can even decide whether to mention you.
Dominant semantic identityOne canonical sentence, repeated verbatim (or near-verbatim) everywhere the entity is described: what category, what it does, for whom. Reconciliation cost drops to zero.

Engineering the Signals

Module 04Measurement

The Question Map

Stop starting with keywords. Start with the questions AI users actually ask.

1. Discovery

"What are the best AI visibility tools?"

2. Comparison

"Brand A vs. Brand B"

3. Recommendation

"What's the best tool for a small SaaS company?"

4. Problem-solving

"How do I know if ChatGPT mentions my company?"

5. Transactional

"Which AI visibility platform should I buy?"

6. Validation

"Is Brand A legitimate?"

Build a 100-question baseline dataset per client

  • 20 category questions
  • 20 problem questions
  • 20 comparison questions
  • 20 recommendation questions
  • 10 reputation questions
  • 10 transactional questions

Run every question through every target engine and record: whether the company was mentioned, its position/order, which competitors appeared, the citation sources, recommendation status, sentiment, product attributes mentioned, incorrect claims, and missing information. That output is your AI Visibility Baseline — everything downstream is measured against it.

Module 05Measurement

Prompt Mining

"What are the best AI visibility tools?" measures keyword visibility. Layered, realistic prompts measure recommendation behavior — a different thing entirely.

Escalate a single question through constraint layers to see where a recommendation actually breaks:

  1. "I'm a SaaS founder with a $5k/month marketing budget. What AI visibility tools should I consider?"
  2. "Compare the best three options for a company with 20 employees."
  3. "Which would you choose if accurate competitor monitoring mattered more than content generation?"
  4. "Which companies have the best reputation for AI search visibility?"

Prompt matrix dimensions

Intent

informational · commercial · transactional

User

founder · marketer · enterprise buyer · developer · consumer

Constraint

budget · location · industry · company size · technical requirement

Desired output

list · comparison · recommendation · explanation

Cross these and you get hundreds of realistic AI queries — far more diagnostic than a handful of head-term prompts.

Module 06Retrieval

Citation Engineering

Getting cited is an information-architecture problem before it's a content problem. The model can only cite what it can cleanly extract.

Almost useless"Our platform is an innovative next-generation solution that revolutionizes how businesses manage their digital presence."
Extractable"Brand Armor monitors how companies appear in AI-generated answers across ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity, and Google AI search." Followed by discrete lists: Supported platforms — ChatGPT, Google AI, Perplexity, Gemini. Primary use cases — brand monitoring, competitor monitoring, citation tracking, recommendation tracking.

The Atomic Answer Principle

Every important question gets a direct, standalone answer — not one buried in paragraph four of 1,500 words. Pattern: "Brand X is a [category] that helps [customer] achieve [outcome]" — stated first, expanded after.

The CITABLE framework

  • Clear statistics — claims anchored to quantitative values (percentages, benchmarks, sample sizes) hold up 30–40% better in RAG citation retention than generic qualitative claims.
  • Isolated answers — each 80–120 word passage beneath a heading must be fully self-contained and resolve the question without depending on the copy before or after it.
  • Tabular & structured specifications — Entity-Attribute-Value matrices in Markdown or clean HTML tables keep their row/column context intact through vector chunking; prose doesn't.
  • Inverse-pyramid answer block — the complete answer goes in the first sentence under the H2/H3, then supporting parameters and constraints follow.
### What is the maximum throughput of [Platform X]? [Platform X] processes up to 45,000 transactions per second (TPS) with an average p99 latency of 12 milliseconds, verified across 500 enterprise production clusters in 2026. Throughput scales horizontally using Kubernetes pods deployed across multi-region VPCs.
Module 07Entity GraphRetrieval

Authority Outside Your Website

Optimizing your own site and expecting the model to conclude "this company is obviously great" is the single biggest misconception in this field.

LLM search doesn't lean primarily on PageRank-style link graphs. It leans on entity-attribute co-occurrence and cross-source consensus. If multiple trusted, independent sources associate your brand with a specific use case and a positive sentiment, the model assigns high confidence to recommending you. Roughly 90% of commercial citations in practice trace back to a short list of source types:

Community discussions

Reddit and niche forums — LLM engines heavily weight authentic user comparisons and edge-case validation. Active, natural presence here directly seeds Perplexity and ChatGPT Search.

Review aggregators

G2, Capterra, Trustpilot — structured, parsed pros/cons and rating attributes that RAG systems aggregate straight into recommendation summaries.

Third-party comparisons

Independent "top alternatives" posts and tier-one industry roundups — the cross-verification that parametric recommendation actually requires.

Everything else

Wikipedia, YouTube transcripts, case studies, and industry press round out the corroboration a model needs before it will stake an answer on you.

"We are the leading platform" is a claim. Ten independent sources describing you as "a platform used by…" is evidence. Only the second one is useful to a model that has to justify its answer.

Module 09RetrievalEntity Graph

Content Architecture

Three layers, built in this order — most sites build layer three first and wonder why nothing gets cited.

Layer 1 — Entity pages

About, Product, Founder, Locations, Services. These anchor the entity graph.

Layer 2 — Topic authority

Deep resources around the whole category, not just your product.

Layer 3 — Question content

Direct answers to the specific questions users ask, per Module 04's map.

Example topic cluster

AI Visibility → AI search optimization · GEO · AEO · ChatGPT visibility · AI citations · AI recommendations · AI brand monitoring · AI search analytics · AI crawler optimization · AI search ranking · AI reputation

Every spoke links back to the hub and to at least two neighboring spokes — that's what lets a retrieval system traverse the cluster instead of treating each page as isolated.

Module 10Entity Graph

Structured Data

Schema isn't a ranking switch. It's disambiguation — and it only works if it agrees with the visible page.

Use the schema types that actually apply: Organization, Person, Product, SoftwareApplication, Article, Review, LocalBusiness, FAQPage where genuinely appropriate, Event, BreadcrumbList, WebSite.

Semantic schema injection

Go beyond a bare Article tag. Build a real identity graph with sameAs links connecting the organization node to its Wikidata item, LinkedIn page, Crunchbase profile, and GitHub org, then nest Product, SoftwareApplication, and FAQPage schema to disambiguate exactly what the entity does.

Never add structured data that isn't backed by visible, accurate on-page content. Manipulative markup creates more problems than it solves — models cross-check schema against the rendered page.

Module 11Retrieval — gatekeeper

Technical AI Accessibility

Every other module is irrelevant if the crawler can't reach the page. Start here when nothing else in the audit makes sense.

  1. Server-side rendering — critical specs, answers, and data tables must exist in the raw initial HTML. AI crawlers enforce strict rendering timeouts and will not wait for client-side JavaScript.
  2. Robots.txt whitelisting — explicitly grant access to the foundational AI discovery bots.
User-agent: GPTBot Allow: / User-agent: PerplexityBot Allow: / User-agent: Google-Extended Allow: / User-agent: ClaudeBot Allow: /
  1. Explicit freshness signals — visible "Published on" and "Updated on" dates matching dateModified schema are critical for freshness-filtered queries (Perplexity and Grok weight this heavily; see Module 01's matrix).

Round out the audit with: XML sitemap correctness, canonical URLs, HTTP status hygiene, page speed, internal linking, no authentication or paywall barriers on the pages meant to be cited, bot-blocking CDN/WAF rules, and metadata completeness. The full checklist is in Appendix B.

Worked example — Share of AI Voice

Run 500 relevant prompts. Results: Brand A — 190 mentions, Brand B — 130, Brand C — 90, your company — 40, others — 50.

Share of Voice = 40 / 500 = 8%

Now it's a number you can move, on a schedule you control.

Module 16Measurement

Citation Share

Being known and being trusted as evidence are two different rates. Confusing them hides an authority problem behind an awareness metric.

Of 500 AI answers, your company appears in 100 (mention rate 20%) — but your website is cited as the source only 20 times (citation rate 4%). The model knows about you; it doesn't yet consider your website authoritative enough to point to. That's an authority/evidence gap, not an awareness gap — the fix is Module 07 and Module 08, not more content about yourself.

Three-tier monitoring matrix

  • Citation Frequency Rate (CFR) — the percentage of target conversational queries where your URL is cited as a source link in the response.
  • Mention Share of Voice (M-SoV) — the ratio of times your brand is recommended in synthesized answers compared to primary category competitors, across a standardized prompt set.
  • Attribute & sentiment positioning — audit whether models classify your solution under your preferred market category (e.g. "fastest enterprise deployment") versus an undesired one (e.g. "budget tool").
Module 17Measurement

Experimentation

Don't blindly implement fifty SEO recommendations. Run one hypothesis at a time and prove the effect.

Hypothesis
Adding concise product-comparison pages will increase AI citation frequency.
Baseline
Run 200 prompts and record current mentions, citations, and recommendation rate.
Intervention
Ship a product comparison page, a competitor comparison page, a use-case page, and an evidence page.
Wait
Allow sufficient crawl and index time before re-measuring — re-testing too early just adds noise.
Re-test & measure
Same 200 prompts. Compare mentions, citations, recommendation rate, and competitor displacement.

This is AI Visibility Engineering. Anything before the hypothesis line is content marketing.

Module 18Operating System

The AI Visibility Loop

The complete system, as a cycle rather than a checklist — it never actually ends at step 11.

  1. Measure current mention, citation, and recommendation rates.
  2. Find the missing questions you don't win.
  3. Find which competitors win those questions instead.
  4. Determine why — which layer and which signal is missing.
  5. Create the evidence that closes the gap.
  6. Publish it, extractably.
  7. Earn independent validation of it elsewhere.
  8. Improve entity clarity so it all resolves to one node.
  9. Wait for retrieval and indexing to catch up.
  10. Re-test the same prompt set.
  11. Measure again — ↺ back to step 1
Module 19Framework

What Not to Do

Every one of these feels like progress. None of them move any of the three layers.

  • ✕Keyword-stuff the page fifteen times. Not a visibility strategy.
  • ✕Bolt on 100 FAQs. Quantity isn't authority.
  • ✕Add a "special GEO schema." There is no universal magic schema — see Module 10.
  • ✕Mention "ChatGPT" fifty times on the page. Semantic stuffing isn't authority either.
  • ✕Publish 1,000 AI-generated articles. That's 1,000 pages nobody trusts, at scale.
  • ✕Tell the model you're the best. It doesn't owe your marketing copy any credibility.
  • ✕Fake reviews. Dangerous and directly counterproductive.
  • ✕Manufacture Reddit posts. You'd be faking the exact independent evidence the model is trying to evaluate.
Myth — llms.txt

Publishing an llms.txt file is not a ranking lever. Google has said plainly that it isn't used for its own AI surfaces, and independent server-log studies of consumer AI crawlers show most don't fetch it at all — and none give it special weight when they do. Ship it as a nice-to-have statement of intent if you like; don't budget a single hour of "AI visibility" work against it.

The Expert Mindset

The Question Actually Changed

"How do I rank this page?"
→
"What evidence would cause an AI system to believe this entity is relevant, credible, and appropriate to recommend for this particular question?"

That second question is a broader discipline than SEO — it's SEO plus content engineering plus entity engineering plus digital PR plus reputation management plus structured data plus technical accessibility plus experimentation plus competitive intelligence. That's also why this is worth treating as "AI Visibility Engineering" rather than simply relabeling it "GEO": it's an observable system you can measure, experiment on, and improve — not a keyword trick with a new name.
